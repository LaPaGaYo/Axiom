## Ground truth (verifier, 2026-11-14, main `f79a8db171`; every fact here was read in the code, the tests or the specs at that commit)

1. **The rule and its modes.** One supervision rule has modes: `stalled` (`src/shared/axiom-contract/supervision.ts`, `StallModeSchema`: `observe`, `notify`, `stop`). It watches headless Workers only. `observe`: a Worker silent past the stall threshold is recorded in the audit, nothing else. `notify`: the same record, and the Attempt shows up in the pending view as a `stall_notice`; the Worker keeps running. `stop`: the Worker is stopped; when the host confirms the stop, its work is preserved where there is any, the Task goes `NEEDS_FIX` and is retried or — out of attempts — `BLOCKED`, and the stop counts toward the Task's `maxAttempts`; a stop the host cannot confirm settles nothing. The mode is read on every supervision pass (every 60 s), so a change applies from the next pass.
2. **Two settings.** The mode in effect for a Mission is its own override if it has one, else the global default, else the built-in `observe`. `axiom.supervision.show {missionId}` (a read) answers `{rules, notifications, evidence, proposals}`; `rules` has exactly one entry: `rule`, `globalMode`, `globalSource` (`built_in`, `user` or `proposal` — where the default comes from), and for a given Mission `missionMode` (the override, or `null`) and `effectiveMode` (the schema marks those two optional: a view asked without a Mission id omits them). `axiom.supervision.update {rule: 'stalled', missionId?, mode}` (desktop only, a mutation with the contract envelope) sets the override when `missionId` is given — `mode: null` clears it — and the global default when it is not (`null` is refused there with `invalid_argument`); an unknown Mission is `mission_not_found`; an unchanged value writes nothing. A change of the global default marks an open proposal `SUPERSEDED`. It answers the same view.
3. **The proposal.** After each reconciliation pass the Control Plane inserts at most one `OPEN` proposal to raise the **global** default one step (`observe` to `notify`, `notify` to `stop`) when enough observed stalls have a known outcome and most of them ended badly; the two thresholds are constants of the main process and are not in any response. `show.proposals` lists the open one (if any) first, then up to five resolved ones (`ACCEPTED`, `DISMISSED`, `SUPERSEDED`); each carries `id`, `fromMode`, `toMode`, `status` and an `evidence` snapshot: `hits` (stalls whose Attempt then timed out, failed or failed verification), `falsePositives` (stalls whose Attempt went on to pass verification), `known` (the two together), `inconclusive`, `hitRate` (`hits / known`, or `null`), `savedMs` (what acting at the threshold would have saved; the reference documentation shows it in minutes, divided by 60 000). `axiom.supervision.resolve {proposalId, resolution: 'accept' | 'dismiss'}` (desktop only, a mutation) accepts — the global default becomes `toMode` — or dismisses; an unknown id is `invalid_argument`, a proposal that is no longer open `illegal_transition`, an accept after the default moved away from `fromMode` `stale_state`. It answers the view without Mission fields. The proposal's `createdAt` / `resolvedAt` are the database's own strings, not ISO.
4. **The pending row.** `axiom.mission.pending` lists the open proposal in every Mission that is not terminal: kind `supervision_proposal`, `objectId` the proposal's id, `reason` `stalled`, not blocking, `actions: ['axiom.supervision.resolve']`, `since` its creation as an ISO time. A terminal Mission's pending view is empty. Mission Home renders the row as text (M3-09-3a: rows carry no controls).
5. **The client.** `src/renderer/src/lib/axiom-client.ts` has no supervision method. It has 270 counted lines of the 300 `max-lines` allows; written the way its neighbours are, the three methods and their import take 30. Thirteen earlier sidecar assertions grep method names in that file, so every method stays on `axiomClient` there.
6. **The page.** `readMission` (`mission-read.ts`) makes the Mission's reads, all started together; the pending view is read once and settles on its own, so that its failure is one line on the page and not a failed Mission read. `useMissionsPageController` (230 lines; a sidecar holds it at 250 or fewer) owns `mutate` and `confirmAction`. `mutate` does nothing and answers `false` while another request is unanswered; otherwise it sends the call with a receipt id, reads the page again whatever the answer, puts a refusal on the page's error line as `code: message` with a toast, and answers whether the call was accepted. `confirmAction` opens the app's confirmation with its title as the confirming button's label; while it is open the page is `busy` and the timed read skips; it resolves to nothing — it does not say whether the action ran or what it returned — and does nothing while another confirmation is open. `MissionsPageSurface.tsx` renders `MissionHome` with the Mission read spread into its props, then the plan panels, the Task table and the Task page. The timed read of M3-09-3b-2 goes through the same `readMission` every five seconds.
7. **How the folder asks a choice.** `VerificationResolveForm.tsx` uses the `RadioGroup` / `RadioGroupItem` / `Label` primitives: its disposition group shows the Control Plane's own word in the mono face with a one-line gloss beside it (`aria-describedby`), its verdict group a consequence paragraph for the chosen value. `TaskCancelForm` hands the controller the consequence text for the confirmation.
8. **Existing tests.** Ten test files of the missions folder mock the client module. Nine of them return only an `axiomClient` with the methods they use — the module's other exports are not on those mocks, and touching `AxiomRpcError` there throws; `TaskEvidenceRead.test.tsx` keeps the real module under its mocked methods. In `MissionPendingRead.test.tsx` a part of the read that failed must raise no `alert`, no page error and no toast; `MissionTimedRefresh.test.tsx` asserts that no `status` is on the page. The controller test stubs the runtime call, rejects a method it does not know, and compares the order of a timed tick's calls with the mount's while the Mission's own answer is held back. `MissionHome.test.tsx` and `MissionHomePending.test.tsx` render `MissionHome` directly with today's props and count its buttons (five).
9. **E2E.** `axiom.supervision.update` and `.show` already go through `desktopCall` in `tests/e2e/mission-stall-stop-continuation.spec.ts`, parsed with `SupervisionShowResponse`. `tests/e2e/mission-home-refresh.spec.ts` sets a Mission up without starting anything (clone, `repo.add`, `axiom.mission.create`, `axiom.plan.propose`) and makes the document visible on the page side with its local `setDocumentVisibility`. No spec produces a proposal: that takes twenty stalls with a known outcome, nine in ten of them hits.

## Outcome

On the Mission's page, right below Mission Home, the person can see which stall mode is in effect for the Mission and why, change the Mission's own setting or the default for all Missions, and accept or dismiss an open upgrade proposal with its evidence in front of them — each through the Control Plane's existing calls, with the consequence stated, and with a confirmation before Workers will be stopped without asking.

## Deliverables

1. **Client.** `axiomClient` gains `showSupervision`, `updateSupervision` and `resolveSupervision` for `axiom.supervision.show`, `.update` and `.resolve`, written like their neighbours: params and responses through the contract schemas of `supervision.ts`, the two mutations with the caller's receipt id in the contract envelope.
2. **The read.** The supervision view is read with the Mission — once per page read, with the selected Mission's id, on the timed read as on the manual one, started together with the Mission's other reads and not after any of them has answered — and apart from the rest: whatever goes wrong in it (a refusal, a transport error, an exception thrown by the call itself) ends as the region's own note and nowhere else — not on the page's error line, not as a toast — and the Mission, its Tasks and its pending items still show.
3. **The region.** Between Mission Home and the plan panels the page renders a region of its own named `Supervision` — not inside the `Mission Home` region — with the rule's label `Stalled Workers` and three facts, each mode in the Control Plane's own word (mono face):
   - `In effect for this Mission`: the effective mode and its gloss — what the Control Plane does in that mode (ground truth 1);
   - `This Mission's setting`: the override, or `default` when there is none;
   - `Default for all Missions`: the global mode and where it comes from — `built in`, `set by you` or `from an accepted proposal`.
   When the supervision read failed the region says `Supervision settings could not be read. Refresh to try again.` as plain text — no `alert` or `status` role — and offers no control.
4. **Changing the mode.** Two quiet buttons, `Change for this Mission` and `Change the default`, each opening one small form inside the region: a radio group (`Stall mode for this Mission` with `default`, `observe`, `notify`, `stop`; `Default stall mode for all Missions` with `observe`, `notify`, `stop`), the current value chosen, every option with its gloss, `default` glossed as using the default for all Missions; a `Save stall mode` button that is disabled until the choice differs from the current value, and `Close`. Saving sends one `axiom.supervision.update` — the Mission form with the Mission's id and the mode (`null` for `default`), the default form without a Mission id — and the page shows the new state only when the read after it answers. After a save the Control Plane accepted the form closes; after a refusal it stays open with the choice. Every control of the region is disabled while the page is `busy`. `Change for this Mission` is not offered for a Mission in a terminal status (the contract's `TERMINAL_MISSION_STATUSES`).
5. **Asking before Workers are stopped without asking.** Saving `stop` in either form first opens the app's confirmation — title `Stop stalled Workers automatically`, the other button `Keep the current mode` — whose text says: from the next supervision pass a silent Worker (of this Mission; or, for the default, of any Mission without its own setting) is stopped without asking; once the stop is confirmed its work is kept and the Task tries again; every stop uses one of the Task's attempts; a Task that runs out of attempts is blocked until the person acts. Declining sends nothing and leaves the form as it was. Leaving `stop` asks nothing.
6. **The proposal.** When the supervision view carries an `OPEN` proposal, the region shows a block named `Upgrade proposal`: that it raises the default for all Missions from `fromMode` to `toMode`; its evidence — how many stalled Attempts have a known outcome, how many of those ended badly and how many went on to pass verification, the share as a whole percent, how many more are inconclusive, and the minutes acting at the threshold would have saved; what accepting does (the default for every Mission without its own setting becomes `toMode`, with that mode's gloss) and what dismissing does (the default stays, outcomes are counted afresh from here, a new proposal can follow). `Accept proposal` and `Dismiss proposal` send one `axiom.supervision.resolve` each; accepting a proposal whose `toMode` is `stop` goes through the confirmation of deliverable 5 first, in the default's wording. The two buttons are offered only while the Mission's pending view lists a `supervision_proposal` for this proposal's id with `axiom.supervision.resolve` among its actions; otherwise the block shows the proposal and says `This proposal cannot be answered from this Mission right now.` Resolved proposals are not shown.
7. **The `refresh` the page gets.** The controller hands out a `refresh` that reads the manual way whatever it is called with (ground truth 10) and still returns the read's promise; the interval keeps depending on the hook's own function, so that it is still installed exactly once.
8. **Catalogs.** Every new text goes through `translate('missions.<key>', '<English>')` with a literal key, and the key is in all seven catalogs (`en` and `zh` with real text; the others may carry the English text). These six keys carry exactly these English texts: `missions.supervision` `Supervision`, `missions.stalledWorkers` `Stalled Workers`, `missions.changeMissionStallMode` `Change for this Mission`, `missions.changeDefaultStallMode` `Change the default`, `missions.acceptProposal` `Accept proposal`, `missions.dismissProposal` `Dismiss proposal`.
