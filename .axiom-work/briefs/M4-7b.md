# Task M4-7b — a person grants or denies the delivery Approval: two desktop-only methods, expiry acted on at the decision, the expiry clock stopped while a Mission is in PAUSED

**Why.** Since M4-7a a Mission that becomes `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` has one `PENDING` delivery Approval bound to the hash of its completion report — and nobody can decide it. This Task adds the two methods a person decides with, `axiom.approval.grant` and `axiom.approval.deny` (desktop only, D78). A grant checks the Approval against the record it was made for and against its expiry; an expired request is closed and requested again, never granted (D70 item 3). A denial blocks the Mission with the reason `delivery_denied` (D70 item 3). And the expiry clock of an Approval stops while its Mission is paused (D70 item 4) — this is the first Task that acts on expiry, so it implements **the Approval part** of that item: the times of a pause and of its end are the `createdAt` of the status events the controller already writes; the wall-clock budget of a Mission (the other clock D70 item 4 names) and the waiting clock of DEF-20 item 1 are not touched. **Nothing is delivered**: a `GRANTED` Approval waits for M4-8, which checks it once more and consumes it. **No interface**: the card with the two buttons is M4-11; until then the methods are reached by tests and specs.

High-risk class: it decides the authorization of delivery.

**The lifecycle is the governing one and this Task does not widen it** (`AGENTS.md`, Human Approval; `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` 6.8; `docs/GLOSSARY.md` 4.10): `PENDING → GRANTED | DENIED | EXPIRED | REVOKED`, then `GRANTED → CONSUMED`. Nothing in this Task changes the status of a `GRANTED` row (the pause clock may move its expiry; that is not a status). If something you need seems to require another move, stop and say so in the report.

**Who decided is never taken from the request** (`AGENTS.md`: "The provenance of a human action comes from the caller, never from a request parameter"; D78 rule 3). The two methods take no `resolver`: the Approval records the resolver `user`, and the audit event of the decision records, as `via`, the kind of the caller that the transport stamped on the call (`desktop` — the only kind these methods accept). `axiom.plan.approve` still reads a `resolver` parameter (D78's revision of 2026-11-14 records that as a narrowing for the handlers that existed then); do not copy that part of it.

## Outcome

A person at the desktop grants or denies a waiting delivery Approval. A grant succeeds only for a `PENDING` delivery Approval of a Mission that is `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`, whose hash is the hash of the Mission's current report and whose expiry has not passed; it records the resolver `user` and how the call came in, and the Mission keeps waiting — now for delivery. A grant of an expired request closes it as `EXPIRED`, requests a new one for the same report, and refuses. A denial records the same and the reason, and blocks the Mission with `delivery_denied`. While a Mission is in the status `PAUSED` the expiry of its Approvals does not run: every move out of `PAUSED` moves it forward by the time just spent there. A granted Mission shows a pending item `delivery_approved`; a denied one shows the existing blocked item. Nothing delivers, nothing consumes, no timer runs, no interface changes.

3. **The decisions** — new file(s) directly under `src/main/axiom/approval/` (this folder is the home of the service `docs/GLOSSARY.md` section 2 names **ApprovalGateway**; say so in the docs and introduce no other name). The time comes from the controller's clock; no clock of its own in this folder.
   - **Grant** of one Approval by id, in this order:
     1. no Approval with that id, or its kind is not `delivery` → refuse `approval_not_found`;
     2. its status is not `PENDING` → refuse `approval_not_pending` (data: the id and the status it has);
     3. its Mission is not `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` → refuse `illegal_transition` (data: `missionId`, `from` the Mission's status, `command` `grant` — or `deny`) — a paused or blocked Mission is resumed or unblocked first, and the request stays `PENDING`;
     4. the Mission's current record is not a `ready` record whose `reportSha256` equals the Approval's `inputsHash` → refuse `approval_stale`; nothing is written (the Control Plane never leaves such a row behind today; a later request or a cancel withdraws it);
     5. its `expiresAt` is not later than the controller's time → **in one write that commits**: the Approval becomes `EXPIRED` (resolver `MissionController`, resolution `expired`), an audit event `approval.expired` is appended, and a new delivery Approval is requested for the same hash with `requestDeliveryApproval` — **and then** the call is refused with `approval_expired` (data: the old id and, as `replacedBy`, the new id). A refusal thrown inside the write would undo the expiry, so it is raised after the write has returned;
     6. otherwise the Approval becomes `GRANTED` (resolver `user`, resolution `granted`), one audit event `approval.granted` is appended (actor `user`, object kind `approval`, object id the Approval's id, `before` holding the status it had, `after` holding the id, the hash, the expiry and `via` — the caller kind the handler read from the RPC context, `createdAt` the controller's time), and the Mission stays `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`. Steps 1–6 of a successful grant are one write.
   - **Deny** of one Approval by id with a reason: a reason that holds a secret is refused before any write, exactly as `rejectPlan` does it (`invalid_argument`, `secret_in_rationale`). Then, in one write: checks 1–3 of the grant (a denial needs no hash or expiry check — refusing delivery is always safe, so a request that is past its time is recorded as `DENIED`, not `EXPIRED`); the Approval becomes `DENIED` (resolver `user`, resolution the reason); one audit event `approval.denied` (actor `user`, same shape, `after` holding the id, the reason and `via`); and the Mission moves to `BLOCKED` with the reason `delivery_denied` through the existing `interruptMission`.
   - Comparing the expiry: parse both sides as dates (a bare date such as `2099-01-01` is a valid expiry).
4. **The pause clock** (D70 item 4, the Approval part). The rule is about the status, not about who paused: **whenever a Mission leaves the status `PAUSED`** — a resume, but also a block or a cancel of a paused Mission — every delivery Approval of that Mission that is `PENDING` or `GRANTED` gets its expiry moved forward by the time the Mission has just spent in `PAUSED`: the controller's time now, minus the `createdAt` of the Mission's latest `mission.status` event whose `after.status` is `PAUSED` (ground truth 10). It is done in the same write as the move, through the store method of deliverable 2, with one audit event `approval.clock_resumed` per Approval (actor `MissionController`, object kind `approval`, `before` holding the old expiry, `after` holding the id, the new expiry as an ISO string and the paused milliseconds). **Wire it in one place**: `moveMission` in `mission-lifecycle.ts` calls the function when the move's source status is `PAUSED`, after it has written the status and its event; the function itself lives in the approval folder, in a file that imports nothing from `mission-lifecycle.ts` (no import cycle). Time in `BLOCKED` never counts — outside a pause or inside one: a Mission that is paused, then blocked, then unblocked and resumed gets the time before the block and the time after the unblock, in two steps. A Mission with no such Approval gets no write and no event.
