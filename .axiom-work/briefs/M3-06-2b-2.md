# Task M3-06-2b-2 — Preserve on the recovery-side endings: stop first, then preserve, then settle (D88 items 1, 2, 3, 7; D88 revision row of 2026-11-11 for item 9(c))

Planner brief, 2026-11-11 (verifier). M3-06-2b preserves the worktree of a headless Attempt that exits without a Submission and lets the next Attempt continue from it — on the exit path only. An Attempt that recovery ends (stopped for its runtime budget, reported stopped by the kernel, or found with its process gone) still loses everything it wrote. **This Task makes recovery preserve such an Attempt with the routine, the audit and the baseline rule of M3-06-2b, in one fixed order: the host first has proof that the Worker's process is gone, then it preserves, then it settles.** Henry approved that order on 2026-11-11 (it replaces "snapshot first, then stop" in D88 item 9(c)); M3-06-3's supervisor stops will go through the same path.

## Outcome

After this Task, when recovery ends a headless Attempt whose process the host has proven gone — it stopped the Worker for its runtime budget, the kernel reports the Worker stopped, or the host observed the process exit — it first preserves what the Worker left, exactly as the exit path does, and only then settles. The next Attempt continues from that snapshot under M3-06-2b's rules and is told how the previous run ended; after a stop it is told that the stopped run is suspect and must be reviewed first. While a stop is unconfirmed, nothing is preserved and nothing is settled. An ending whose process is not proven gone is settled as today, without a snapshot.

## Deliverables

1. **The order.** In (d), the capture runs after a `stopped` receipt and before the settlement transaction; a `stop_unknown` receipt changes nothing compared with main (no capture, no settlement). In (b), a headless Attempt whose worker state is `stopped` is captured before its transaction. In (c), a headless Attempt is captured before its transaction. Nowhere is a worktree captured while the host still counts the Worker as live or unverifiable. TUI Attempts, planner bindings, (b) with `failed` / `abandoned`, (e), launch orphans and cancellation are settled exactly as on main, without a capture.
2. **Capture in recovery** (new module; async; never throws). It obtains the launch record, the provider and the event text the way the exit path does and calls `collectAttemptPreservation` unchanged. The launch record is resolved and checked against the kernel worker row and the binding by **one function shared with the exit path** — extract those lines from `submitExit`, do not copy them; the exit path keeps its behaviour and its test files stay untouched. When the record is missing, unreadable or does not match, nothing is captured, no `attempt.preserved` is written and the settlement proceeds as on main. Failure semantics: no error of the capture reaches `reconcileAttempt`'s caller; a settlement never fails, waits beyond the routine's own git timeouts or is retried because of it; nothing is deleted or reset.
3. **Settle with the result.** The capture is awaited outside any database transaction. After it, durable authority is read again: when the Attempt is no longer the Task's current unsubmitted one, or its worker row no longer satisfies the branch, **nothing of this branch is written** and the binding is reconciled again from the top. Otherwise `attempt.preserved` (the payload of ground truth 3, written through the recovery audit helper) goes into the same transaction as the branch's settlement: both rows or neither.
4. **Endings say how they ended.** (b) writes `attempt.stopped { reason: 'kernel_stopped' }` for a worker state `stopped`, in the settlement transaction (both channels; the reason says the kernel reported the stop and Axiom's own stop did not get to record it). (c) adds `settlement: { kind: 'failed' }` to the `attempt.failed` of a headless Attempt of the same runtime; the restart ending and TUI Attempts keep their rows. Settlement reasons, stage transitions, refunds and `maxAttempts` accounting do not change anywhere.
5. **One snapshot per Attempt, whoever asks.** Two preservations of the same Attempt that overlap (the exit path and recovery, or a pass replayed after a crash) end with the same preserved commit: the later one adopts the ref the earlier one published, under the existing message rule, instead of reporting `git_failure`. A ref holding another commit stays `ref_conflict`.
6. **Baseline.** A preserved Attempt that ended by a stop is usable under the same conditions as today (preserved, no secret finding, every scope path within the Task's current `allowedPaths`). Its ending is taken from its own rows: an `attempt.failed` that parses as today wins; otherwise its `attempt.stopped` gives `{ kind: 'stopped', reason }`. When its snapshot is not usable, the walk treats it as it treats a `failed` predecessor today (it ends there, integration base) and the result says why its work was not used. `SnapshotAttemptBaseline` carries what deliverable 7 needs for the other two recovery endings. **Without an `attempt.preserved` row every result is deep-equal to today's** — `attempt-baseline.test.ts` and `previous-attempt.test.ts` pass unmodified.
7. **Context** (`previous-preserved-attempt.ts` and what it calls; from records only). The statement about how the previous Attempt ended is true for each recovery ending and stays inside the mandatory part of the section (the 3 KiB rule of M3-06-2b is unchanged):
   - **stopped**: that the Attempt was stopped before it could submit, with the recorded reason; that a stopped run is suspect — its direction may have been wrong or stuck — and that what it left must be reviewed before it is built on (D88 item 2).
   - **interrupted by a restart** (`evidence: 'runtime_restarted'`): that the runtime which started it restarted and its process was gone afterwards — not "interrupted by unknown".
   - **process gone without an exit marker** (the same-runtime ending of (c)): that its process ended without an exit marker and the cause is not known — it must not say that the Attempt failed on its own error.
   The existing statements for the exit path's `interrupted` and `failed` endings stay as they are. The wording is yours; name no destructive git command.
8. **Launch.** Nothing new: a snapshot baseline from a stopped Attempt launches and is audited like any other (`continued` / `not_continued`).
9. **Stub and E2E** (host, verifier-run; you cannot run Electron). The headless Codex stub gains an opt-in stall, in the helper file M3-06-2b added for partial work: with marker `<ledger>.stall-<taskId>` present, after the hold is released it removes that marker, leaves the partial work named by `<ledger>.partial-<taskId>` (the existing step, with its message and command events), and then stays alive without finishing its turn. `withFixtureMission` gains a way to give the fixture Tasks a runtime budget (mind ground truth 8: the helper is at the limit). Expected: Attempt 1 gets `attempt.stopped` with reason `runtime_budget_exceeded` and an `attempt.preserved` that says preserved and names the path; in the clone the ref resolves to the audited commit; **Attempt 1's worktree still contains the file after the stop** (find the worktree through `git worktree list` in the clone); Attempt 2 starts by itself; its `attempt.started` audit says it continued Attempt 1's snapshot; its `prompt.md` contains the preserved commit sha, the path, the stub's message and command texts and the recorded stop reason; Attempt 2 submits and the tree of its Candidate commit contains the file. ## Non-goals

Preserving or reclassifying an Attempt whose process is **not** proven gone: (b) with kernel state `abandoned` — including the restart that also lost the daemon (ground truth 9) — or `failed`, and (e) abandonment, also after `stop_unknown`; a grace phase or any other change to how a Worker is stopped (M3-06-3); any change to which path settles an Attempt, to settlement reasons, refunds, the ladder, the chain of three, the cooldown or `maxAttempts`; TUI Attempts and planner bindings; declared and unchanged-Candidate exits; resuming a harness session (M3-06-2c); supervisor rules (M3-06-3); deleting or expiring preserved refs; a command, RPC, CLI verb or UI; Policy keys; tables or migrations; changes to verification, the Scheduler or the outbox; Windows / WSL.
