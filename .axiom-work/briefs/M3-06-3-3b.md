# Task M3-06-3-3b — Rule supervision, fifth part: enforcing the token budget (D88 rule 10 and revision ③ of 2026-11-12; second half of the token budget)

Planner brief, 2026-11-12 (verifier). M3-06-3-3 measures each headless Worker Attempt's new tokens into the ledger `attempt_token_usage` and shows them through `axiom.mission.usage`. This Task enforces Henry's rule (D88 revision ③): `task.spec.budget.maxTokens` caps a whole Task — all its Attempts together; at the cap the running Attempt is stopped and preserved, it ends as a counted failure with the reason `budget_exhausted`, and the Task waits in `BLOCKED` until a person raises the cap with `task.adjust`; `policy.budget.maxTokens` caps the whole Mission and is checked at admission, like `maxWallClockMinutes`; every launch prompt states the remaining budget. It also fixes two ledger rules the M3-06-3-3 verification flagged.

## Outcome

After this Task a Task with `budget.maxTokens` cannot spend past it unnoticed: each pass, the supervision step reads the running Attempt's new tokens, and when the Task's total reaches the cap it stops the Attempt the way the runtime limit does, preserves its work, settles it as a counted failure with the reason `budget_exhausted` and parks the Task in `BLOCKED` until a person raises the cap with `task.adjust`; a READY Task already at its cap is blocked at admission instead of launched; a Mission whose total reached `policy.budget.maxTokens` is blocked at admission like any other Mission budget; every headless launch prompt of a capped Task or Mission states what remains, without breaking an in-place resume. Readings no longer drop, and rate-limit-only rollout records no longer make a reading partial.

## Deliverables

1. **Ledger rules.** (a) The Codex reader skips a `token_count` record whose `info` is `null`; a record with `info` but no usable `last_token_usage` inside the window still makes the reading `partial`. (b) Writing a reading over an existing non-final row never lowers it: each count becomes the larger of the stored and the read value, and the row becomes `partial` when any read count is lower than the stored one or the reading is `unavailable` while the row has numbers. This holds for the per-pass update, the supervision step's write (2) and finalization; a final row still never changes. Put each rule where the ledger group of the red check can revert it — (a) in the reader, and (b) most naturally in the store's upsert, since every write, the supervision step's included, goes through it.
2. **Token rule in the supervision step.** In `superviseAttempt`, after the stall rule and the `timeout` rule, for a headless Worker Attempt whose Task has `budget.maxTokens` (`attempt-reconciliation.ts` passes the cap in the supervision input — at most 3 added lines there): read the Attempt now with the existing reader for its provider; when it is still the current Attempt after the read, write its ledger row as the metering step would (the rules of 1 apply), then take the Task's ledger total (all its Attempts, this row included). When that total is at or above the cap, stop through `stopSupervisedAttempt` with the facts `{ rule: 'budget_exhausted', mode: 'auto', thresholdTokens, usedTokens, thresholdSource: 'task' }` (`usedTokens` is that total). The check does not depend on the reading's status: an Attempt whose usage cannot be read adds nothing, but the Task's other Attempts still count. The order is stall, then timeout, then tokens; one rule acts at most once per pass.
3. **Settlement of a token stop.** In one transaction named `recovery_token_stop`, with the guard of `settleAttemptWithoutSubmission` (an old pass cannot settle a newer admission or a submitted Attempt): `attempt.supervised { …facts, action }`, then `attempt.stopped { reason: 'budget_exhausted' }`, `attempt.preserved` when captured, and the Task `RUNNING → NEEDS_FIX` (reason `budget_exhausted`, actor `RecoveryService`) and `NEEDS_FIX → BLOCKED` (reason `token_budget_exhausted`) whatever the remaining attempts — a retry would only be stopped again; or, when the stop is unconfirmed, `attempt.supervised` and `attempt.stop_unknown { reason: 'budget_exhausted' }` only. This is the Decision's "Attempt FAILED `budget_exhausted` (counted), Task `BLOCKED(budget)`": the Attempt is a counted work failure like `timeout` (its projected state stays `STOPPED`), and the `BLOCKED` reason names the token budget so it is not confused with `retry_budget_exhausted` or the Mission's `budget_exhausted`. The timeout path keeps `recovery_runtime_budget` and the stall stop `recovery_stall_stop`; their audits and stage moves do not change.
4. **Admission.** (a) Task: in `check()`, after the Mission's budget, a READY Task with `budget.maxTokens` whose ledger total is at or above the cap is moved `READY → BLOCKED` (reason `token_budget_exhausted`, actor `Scheduler`) with `budget.exhausted { dimension: 'maxTokens', limit, used }` on the Task (object kind `task`) in the same transaction, and skipped with the reason `task_tokens`; the round goes on with the other Tasks. (b) Mission: `budgetExhaustion` gains the dimension `maxTokens` after the existing two — the Mission's ledger total at or above `policy.budget.maxTokens` blocks the Mission `budget_exhausted` through the existing path (`budget.exhausted` on the Mission, `blocked: 'maxTokens'` in the round). Running Attempts are not stopped by the Mission cap. (c) A Planner start is refused with `budget_exhausted` (data `{ reason: 'budget_exhausted' }`) when the Mission's ledger total reached its cap, like `maxAttempts`. `scheduler.ts` may gain at most 8 lines (at most 12 added); put the Task check in a new module.
5. **`task.adjust maxTokens`.** `TaskAdjustParams` gains `maxTokens` (positive integer, optional). `adjustTask` refuses a value at or below the Task's ledger total with `invalid_argument` (data `taskId`, `used`, `maxTokens`) and writes nothing; otherwise it stores it as `spec.budget.maxTokens` (the other budget fields kept), and `task.adjusted` (`before.maxTokens` the previous cap or `null`, `after.maxTokens` the new one) and `plan.minor_adjustment` (`after.maxTokens`) carry the field only when the call sets it. A Task blocked by its token budget then leaves `BLOCKED` through the existing path — to its previous stage, and from `NEEDS_FIX` to `READY` when attempts remain; otherwise the existing `retry_budget_exhausted` refusal applies, so a Task out of both budgets needs both raised in one call. A call without `maxTokens` leaves the cap as it is; a Task still at its cap is blocked again at its next admission.
6. **Remaining budget in prompts.** A headless Attempt's package gains a mandatory section `Token budget`, right after "Permissions, budget and submission contract", when its Task has `budget.maxTokens` or its Mission has `policy.budget.maxTokens`. For the Task: the cap, the Task's ledger total when the package is built, what remains (not below 0), that the count is new tokens (input not served from cache, cache writes and output) across all the Task's Attempts, and that at the cap the host stops the Attempt, preserves its work and blocks the Task until a person raises the budget. For the Mission: its cap, total and remainder, and that at the cap no new Attempt starts. `sameContract` skips this section as it skips "Previous-Attempt failure evidence" (at most 4 changed lines in `in-place-continuation.ts`), so a changed usage alone never ends an in-place resume, while a changed cap still changes the compared "Permissions" section. A Codex in-place continuation prompt (`continuationPrompt`; its caller passes the caps and totals, at most 10 changed lines in `headless-worker-launch.ts`) gains the same lines. Without a cap nothing changes in either prompt, and TUI packages never get the section (their Attempts are neither measured nor stopped).
